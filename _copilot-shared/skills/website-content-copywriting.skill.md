# Skill: Website Content and Copywriting

## Purpose

This skill helps **complete beginners** write effective website content —
from headlines and service descriptions to about pages and calls to action.

It is for people who have never written website copy before and need practical,
actionable guidance on making their content clear, compelling, and appropriate
for their audience.

---

## Audience

- Complete beginners who need to write content for their website.
- Small business owners who cannot afford a professional copywriter.
- Anyone who feels stuck staring at a blank page trying to write their "About"
  section.

---

## The Fundamental Truth About Website Content

**People don't read websites. They scan.**

Research consistently shows that web visitors:

- Read approximately 20–28% of the words on a page.
- Scan in an F-shaped pattern (top, then partway across, then down the left side).
- Decide within 3–5 seconds whether to stay or leave.
- Are looking for specific information, not browsing for pleasure.

Every sentence on your website must earn its place. If it doesn't help the
visitor find what they need or take the action you want, remove it.

---

## Before You Write: Questions to Answer

Before writing any page, answer these questions:

1. **Who is reading this page?** (Be specific: "busy parent looking for a local
   plumber" not "everyone.")
2. **What do they need to know?** (What question brought them here?)
3. **What do you want them to do after reading?** (Call you? Buy? Sign up? Visit?)
4. **What objection might stop them?** (Too expensive? Too far? Not trustworthy?)

Write the answers down. Refer to them while writing. If a sentence doesn't serve
one of these purposes, delete it.

## Homepage Content

The homepage is usually the most visited page. It has one job: **tell visitors
they're in the right place and guide them to the next step.**

### Homepage Structure

1. **Headline** — What you do, for whom, in one sentence.
2. **Subheadline** — The key benefit or what makes you different.
3. **Primary call to action** (CTA) — The one thing you most want visitors to do.
4. **Brief overview** — 2–3 short sections covering key offerings or benefits.
5. **Trust signals** — Reviews, testimonials, certifications, client logos.
6. **Secondary CTA** — Repeat the action or offer an alternative (learn more,
   browse services).

### Homepage Mistakes to Avoid

- "Welcome to our website" — tells the visitor nothing useful.
- Talking about yourself before explaining how you help them.
- Multiple competing CTAs with equal visual weight.
- Large blocks of unbroken text.
- Stock photos that look generic (real photos build more trust).

## About Page Content

The "About" page is often the second most-visited page. Visitors go there to
decide: **"Can I trust these people?"**

### About Page Structure

1. **Who you are** — Name, role, brief background (relevant to your service).
2. **Why you do this** — Your motivation or story (keep it concise).
3. **Who you help** — Describe your ideal client/customer.
4. **What makes you different** — Qualifications, experience, approach.
5. **Photo** — A real photo of you or your team (builds trust significantly more
   than stock images).
6. **CTA** — How to get in touch or work with you.

### About Page Mistakes to Avoid

- Writing your entire life story.
- Using corporate jargon ("synergy," "leverage," "holistic solutions").
- Not including a photo (people trust faces more than text).
- Forgetting a call to action (what should they do after reading?).

## Service/Product Descriptions

Each service or product page should answer: **"What is this, is it for me, and
what happens next?"**

### Structure for Service Pages

1. **What the service is** — Plain English, one sentence.
2. **Who it's for** — "This is ideal if you..."
3. **What's included** — Bullet points of what they get.
4. **How it works** — Simple 3–5 step process.
5. **Pricing** (if possible) — Even a range helps ("from £99").
6. **FAQ** — Address common objections.
7. **CTA** — How to book, buy, or enquire.

### Tips for Service Descriptions

- Focus on **benefits** (what the customer gets) not **features** (what you do).
  - Feature: "We use diamond-tipped blades."
  - Benefit: "Your worktops will be cut perfectly straight with no chipping."
- Use the customer's language, not industry jargon.
- Include specifics where possible (numbers, timescales, areas served).

## Headlines and Headings

Headlines are the most-read element on any page. Many visitors read the headline
and nothing else — then decide whether to stay.

### Rules for Headlines

1. **Be specific.** "Plumbing Services in Bristol" beats "Welcome to Our Services."
2. **Lead with the benefit.** "Get Hot Water Back Today" beats "Emergency Plumber."
3. **Use plain language.** Write as you'd speak to a customer face-to-face.
4. **Keep it short.** 6–12 words maximum for main headlines.
5. **Include who it's for** when possible. "Wedding Photography for Relaxed
   Couples" is clearer than "Photography Services."

### Heading Hierarchy

Use headings (H1, H2, H3) to create a scannable structure:

- **H1** — One per page, the main topic. What the page is about.
- **H2** — Major sections within the page.
- **H3** — Sub-topics within each section.

A visitor should be able to understand what the page covers by reading
only the headings.

## Calls to Action (CTAs)

A CTA is the instruction that tells the visitor what to do next. Every page
needs at least one.

### CTA Principles

1. **Be specific.** "Book a Free Consultation" beats "Submit."
2. **Use action verbs.** "Get," "Book," "Download," "Start," "Join."
3. **Create urgency gently.** "Book this week" or "Limited availability" — but
   never fake urgency.
4. **Make it visible.** A CTA should stand out visually (button, contrasting
   colour, clear placement).
5. **One primary CTA per page.** Multiple equal CTAs create decision paralysis.

### CTA Examples by Business Type

| Business Type | Weak CTA | Strong CTA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plumber | "Contact Us" | "Get a Free Quote Today" |
| Restaurant | "Click Here" | "Book a Table for Tonight" |
| Coach | "Submit" | "Book Your Free Discovery Call" |
| eCommerce | "Buy" | "Add to Basket — Free Delivery Over £50" |
| Newsletter | "Subscribe" | "Get Weekly Tips — Join 2,000+ Readers" |

## Writing Tone and Voice

### For Most Small Business Websites

- **Conversational** — write as you'd speak to a customer across a table.
- **Clear** — short sentences, common words, no jargon.
- **Confident** — state what you do without hedging ("We fix boilers" not "We
  may be able to potentially assist with boiler-related issues").
- **Warm but professional** — friendly without being overly casual.

### Adjust for Your Audience

- **Professional services (legal, financial):** Slightly more formal, but still
  clear. Avoid legalese unless writing actual legal content.
- **Creative businesses:** More personality, storytelling, visual language.
- **Technical services:** Clear explanations, avoid assuming knowledge.
- **Local trades:** Direct, practical, no-nonsense.

### Consistency

Pick a tone and maintain it across all pages. Inconsistency (formal on the
homepage, casual on the blog) confuses visitors about who you are.

## Content Length Guidelines

| Page Type | Recommended Length | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage | 300–800 words | Quick orientation, not detailed reading |
| About page | 300–600 words | Enough to build trust, not a memoir |
| Service page | 400–1,000 words | Answer all questions, address objections |
| Blog post | 800–2,000 words | Detailed enough to be useful for SEO |
| Contact page | 50–150 words | Just tell them how to reach you |
| FAQ page | As needed | Answer real questions your customers ask |

These are guidelines, not rules. A complex service may need 2,000 words. A
simple product may need 200. Write as much as needed, then cut everything
that doesn't serve the reader.

## Formatting for the Web

### Rules for Readable Web Content

1. **Short paragraphs** — 2–4 sentences maximum. One idea per paragraph.
2. **Subheadings every 2–3 paragraphs** — break content into scannable chunks.
3. **Bullet points** for lists, features, or steps.
4. **Bold key phrases** — helps scanners find what they need.
5. **White space** — don't crowd text. Let it breathe.
6. **One idea per sentence** — simple sentences are easier to scan.
7. **Front-load important information** — put the conclusion first, then explain.

### What to Avoid

- Walls of unbroken text.
- Sentences longer than 25 words.
- Paragraphs longer than 5 lines on screen.
- ALL CAPS (reads as shouting).
- Excessive exclamation marks.
- Underlined text (looks like a link and confuses visitors).

## SEO Content Basics

To help search engines understand your content:

1. **Include your main keyword naturally** in the page title, H1, first
   paragraph, and a few subheadings.
2. **Write for humans first, search engines second.** If it reads unnaturally,
   remove the keyword.
3. **Use related terms** — don't repeat the same phrase 20 times. Use synonyms
   and related concepts.
4. **Answer the search question directly.** If someone searches "how to unblock
   a drain," your page should answer that clearly.
5. **Write useful meta descriptions** (the snippet that appears in search
   results) — 150–160 characters summarising the page.

## Content You Can Write Yourself

Even without a professional copywriter, you can write:

- **FAQ answers** — you know what customers ask you every day.
- **Service descriptions** — you know what you do and what's included.
- **About page** — you know your own story.
- **Blog posts about your expertise** — write what you'd tell a customer.
- **Testimonial requests** — ask customers specific questions rather than
  "please leave a review."

### When to Hire a Copywriter

Consider professional help if:

- You're a larger business with significant marketing budget.
- You need a complete website rewrite.
- Your industry has complex messaging or heavy competition.
- You've tried writing and consistently struggle.
- You need sales pages, ad copy, or email sequences.

Typical costs: £100–500 per page, £1,000–5,000 for a full website, depending on
complexity and the writer's experience.

## Content Maintenance

Website content is not "set and forget."

| Frequency | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly | Check for outdated information (prices, hours, team members) |
| Quarterly | Review and update service/product descriptions |
| Biannually | Refresh "About" page and key landing pages |
| Annually | Full content audit — is everything still accurate and relevant? |
| On every change | Update affected pages immediately (new price, new service, staff departure) |

---

## Content Checklist for Launch

Before going live:

- [ ] Every page has a clear purpose and CTA.
- [ ] Headlines are specific and benefit-focused.
- [ ] Content is written in the customer's language, not industry jargon.
- [ ] All facts, prices, and contact details are current and accurate.
- [ ] Content is formatted for scanning (short paragraphs, subheadings, bullets).
- [ ] Each page answers: who is this for, what do they get, what should they
      do next?
- [ ] Spelling and grammar have been checked.
- [ ] Someone other than the author has read it and understood it.

---

## Critical Constraints

- Never use placeholder text ("Lorem ipsum") on a live site.
- Never copy content from another website (plagiarism and SEO penalty).
- Never make claims you cannot substantiate (legal risk in advertising standards).
- Never promise results you cannot guarantee ("Lose 10kg in a week!").
- Never use fake testimonials or reviews.
- Never ignore spelling and grammar — it affects trust.
- Always write for the visitor's needs, not your ego.
- Always have someone else proofread before going live.
- Always keep content up to date — outdated content damages trust.
