# Skill: Website Privacy and Legal Compliance

## Purpose

This skill covers **privacy law, cookie consent, accessibility legislation, and
legal requirements** for public websites — across all major jurisdictions
worldwide.

It is written for **complete beginners** who have no legal background and may not
know that their website is subject to laws in countries they've never visited.

**Important:** This skill provides general guidance to help identify obligations.
It is NOT legal advice. For complex situations, consult a qualified lawyer in
the relevant jurisdiction(s).

---

## Audience

- Complete beginners building their first website.
- Small business owners who need to comply with privacy law but cannot afford
  a legal team.
- Anyone whose website has visitors from multiple countries.

---

## The Fundamental Principle

**If your website is accessible to people in a country, that country's privacy
and accessibility laws may apply to you — regardless of where YOU are located.**

A small business in Brisbane with a website that EU residents can visit must
comply with GDPR. A sole trader in London whose site is used by Californians
must comply with CPRA. Geography is about where your VISITORS are, not where
your server is.

---

## How to Determine Which Laws Apply

Ask these questions:

1. **Where are your visitors located?** Check analytics (if available) or
   consider: who is your target audience, and could anyone else access the site?
2. **Do you specifically target certain countries?** (e.g. pricing in their
   currency, advertising in their market, content in their language)
3. **Do you collect any personal data?** (Names, emails, IP addresses, device
   fingerprints, location data, browsing behaviour — all count.)
4. **Do you use cookies or similar tracking technology?**
5. **Does your site have special categories of visitors?** (Children, patients,
   students — additional protections apply.)

If the answer to questions 3 or 4 is "yes" AND your visitors come from regulated
jurisdictions, compliance is required.

## Privacy Laws by Jurisdiction

### EU / EEA — General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

| Aspect | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Who it applies to** | Any website that processes personal data of people in the EU/EEA — regardless of where the website owner is located |
| **What counts as personal data** | Name, email, IP address, device ID, location data, cookies, any information that can identify a person directly or indirectly |
| **Consent requirement** | Explicit, informed, freely given consent BEFORE collecting non-essential data |
| **Cookie rules** | Only essential cookies (those needed for the site to function) may be set without consent. All others require explicit opt-in |
| **Privacy policy** | Must be clear, in plain language, and explain: what data is collected, why, how long it's kept, who has access, data subject rights |
| **Data subject rights** | Access, rectification, erasure ("right to be forgotten"), portability, objection, restriction |
| **Data breach notification** | Must notify supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware |
| **Penalties** | Up to €20 million or 4% of global annual turnover (whichever is higher) |
| **Key practical actions** | Cookie consent banner (opt-in), privacy policy, data processing records, DPA with processors |

### United Kingdom — UK GDPR + PECR

| Aspect | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Who it applies to** | Any website processing personal data of UK residents |
| **Differences from EU GDPR** | Nearly identical in practice; enforced by the ICO (Information Commissioner's Office); UK has its own adequacy decisions |
| **Cookie rules (PECR)** | Same as EU — explicit consent before non-essential cookies |
| **Privacy policy** | Same requirements as EU GDPR |
| **Penalties** | Up to £17.5 million or 4% of global turnover |
| **Key practical actions** | Same as EU GDPR — treat as equivalent |

### California, USA — CPRA (California Privacy Rights Act)

| Aspect | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Who it applies to** | Businesses that: collect personal information of California residents AND meet revenue/data thresholds (>$25M revenue, or buy/sell data of 100,000+ consumers, or derive 50%+ revenue from selling data) |
| **Consent model** | Opt-OUT (not opt-in) — you can collect data but must allow users to say "don't sell/share my data" |
| **Required disclosures** | "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link; privacy policy listing categories of data collected and purposes |
| **Cookie rules** | No specific cookie consent requirement, but sale/sharing of data (including through cookies) requires opt-out mechanism |
| **Penalties** | $2,500 per unintentional violation, $7,500 per intentional violation |
| **Key practical actions** | "Do Not Sell" link, privacy policy, honour opt-out requests within 15 business days |

### Brazil — LGPD (Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados)

| Aspect | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Who it applies to** | Any organisation processing personal data of people in Brazil, regardless of where the organisation is based |
| **Consent model** | Consent must be free, informed, and unambiguous; can also process under "legitimate interest" |
| **Cookie rules** | Consent generally required for non-essential cookies |
| **Data subject rights** | Confirmation, access, correction, anonymisation, portability, deletion, information about sharing |
| **Penalties** | Up to 2% of revenue in Brazil (capped at R$50 million per violation) |
| **Key practical actions** | Cookie consent, privacy policy in Portuguese (if targeting Brazil), DPO appointment in some cases |

### Australia — Privacy Act 1988 + Australian Privacy Principles (APPs)

| Aspect | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Who it applies to** | Organisations with annual turnover >A$3 million, plus health service providers, and some small businesses handling personal information |
| **Consent model** | No specific cookie consent requirement, but must disclose collection in privacy policy |
| **Cookie rules** | No explicit cookie law, but collecting personal information via cookies must be disclosed and have a lawful purpose |
| **Privacy policy** | Must be freely available, describe what information is collected, why, how it's stored, and how to access/correct it |
| **Penalties** | Up to A$50 million for serious/repeated breaches (post-2022 amendments) |
| **Key practical actions** | Privacy policy (even if no cookie banner is legally required, transparency is expected), data breach notification scheme |

### South Africa — POPIA (Protection of Personal Information Act)

| Aspect | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Who it applies to** | Any person or organisation processing personal information of South African data subjects |
| **Consent model** | Consent or another justification (legitimate interest, legal obligation, contract) |
| **Cookie rules** | Consent generally required for tracking cookies |
| **Data subject rights** | Access, correction, deletion, objection |
| **Penalties** | Fines up to R10 million and/or imprisonment up to 10 years for serious offences |
| **Key practical actions** | Cookie consent, privacy policy, information officer registration |

### Singapore — PDPA (Personal Data Protection Act)

| Aspect | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Who it applies to** | Organisations collecting, using, or disclosing personal data of individuals in Singapore |
| **Consent model** | Consent required; may be deemed consent in some contexts |
| **Cookie rules** | Consent required for personal data collection via cookies |
| **Penalties** | Up to S$1 million or 10% of annual turnover |
| **Key practical actions** | Consent mechanism, privacy policy, DPO appointment, data breach notification within 3 days |

### Canada — PIPEDA (Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act)

| Aspect | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Who it applies to** | Private-sector organisations handling personal information in commercial activities in Canada |
| **Consent model** | Meaningful consent; can be express or implied depending on sensitivity |
| **Cookie rules** | Implied consent generally acceptable for basic analytics; express consent for sensitive data or targeted advertising |
| **Penalties** | Up to C$100,000 per violation (under current law; proposed Bill C-27 would increase significantly) |
| **Key practical actions** | Privacy policy, consent mechanism for sensitive data, breach notification |

### Other Jurisdictions

| Country/Region | Key Law | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Thailand** | PDPA (2022) | Similar to GDPR; consent required |
| **India** | DPDP Act (2023) | Consent-based; significant penalties |
| **Japan** | APPI | Consent for sensitive data; opt-out for some uses |
| **South Korea** | PIPA | Strict consent requirements; GDPR-comparable |
| **New Zealand** | Privacy Act 2020 | Similar to Australian approach |
| **Nigeria** | NDPR | Consent-based; privacy policy required |
| **Kenya** | Data Protection Act | Consent-based; data localisation considerations |

## Cookie Consent: Practical Implementation

### When You Need a Cookie Consent Banner

You NEED one if:

- Your site uses non-essential cookies (analytics, marketing, social media
  embeds, personalisation) AND
- You have visitors from the EU, UK, Brazil, or other consent-required
  jurisdictions.

You probably DON'T need one if:

- Your site uses only essential cookies (session, security, basic functionality) AND
- You use a cookie-free analytics tool (Plausible, Fathom, etc.) AND
- You don't embed third-party tracking scripts.

### Cookie Consent Requirements (EU/UK)

1. **Pre-consent blocking:** Non-essential cookies must NOT be set until the user
   explicitly clicks "Accept." Loading Google Analytics before consent = violation.
2. **Granular choice:** Users must be able to accept some categories and reject
   others (not just "Accept All" / "Reject All").
3. **No dark patterns:** The "Reject" option must be as easy to find and use as
   "Accept." No hidden settings, tiny text, or guilt-tripping.
4. **Revocable:** Users must be able to change their choice later.
5. **Documented:** Keep records of what consent was given and when.
6. **Renewed:** Consent expires — ask again after 6–12 months.

## Privacy Policy: What to Include

Every website that collects ANY personal data needs a privacy policy. It must
be written in plain language (not legalese) and cover:

1. **Who you are** — business name, contact details, data protection officer
   (if applicable).
2. **What data you collect** — list every type (names, emails, IP addresses,
   cookies, device info, location).
3. **How you collect it** — forms, cookies, analytics, third-party services.
4. **Why you collect it** — the legal basis (consent, contract, legitimate
   interest).
5. **Who you share it with** — third parties, sub-processors (Google Analytics,
   email marketing tool, hosting provider).
6. **Where data is stored** — which country/region (matters for international
   transfers).
7. **How long you keep it** — retention periods for each data type.
8. **Security measures** — how you protect the data.
9. **User rights** — what rights visitors have and how to exercise them.
10. **How to contact you** — email or form for data requests.
11. **How to complain** — link to the relevant supervisory authority.
12. **When the policy was last updated.**

### Common Mistakes

- Using a template without customising it to your actual data practices.
- Listing data you don't actually collect (misleading).
- Failing to mention third-party services (Google Analytics sends data to the US).
- Making the policy inaccessible (must be linked from every page, typically in
  the footer).

## Accessibility Legislation

Accessibility law requires websites to be usable by people with disabilities —
including those using screen readers, keyboard navigation, voice control, or
other assistive technologies.

### Key Accessibility Laws

| Law | Jurisdiction | Scope | Standard | Deadline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **ADA** (Americans with Disabilities Act) | USA | Public accommodations, state/local government | WCAG 2.1 AA (via case law) | Already applicable |
| **Section 508** | USA (federal) | Federal agencies and their suppliers | WCAG 2.0 AA | Already applicable |
| **EAA** (European Accessibility Act) | EU | Products and services (including websites) of private-sector companies | EN 301 549 (≈ WCAG 2.1 AA) | 28 June 2025 |
| **EN 301 549** | EU | Public sector websites | WCAG 2.1 AA | Already applicable |
| **Equality Act 2010** | UK | Service providers (interpreted to include websites) | WCAG 2.1 AA (practical standard) | Already applicable |
| **DDA** (Disability Discrimination Act) | Australia | Service providers | WCAG 2.0 AA (via case law) | Already applicable |
| **AODA** (Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act) | Ontario, Canada | Organisations with 50+ employees | WCAG 2.0 AA | Already applicable |

### What WCAG 2.1 AA Requires (Simplified)

WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines) is the international standard for
web accessibility. Level AA is the most commonly required level.

Key requirements at a beginner level:

- **Text alternatives** — every image has descriptive alt text.
- **Keyboard navigation** — everything can be reached without a mouse.
- **Colour contrast** — text has sufficient contrast against its background
  (4.5:1 minimum for normal text).
- **Resizable text** — content remains usable at 200% zoom.
- **Headings and structure** — content uses proper heading hierarchy.
- **Form labels** — every form field has a visible, associated label.
- **Error identification** — form errors are clearly described in text.
- **Focus visible** — keyboard users can see which element is currently focused.
- **Captions** — videos have captions for deaf/hard-of-hearing users.
- **No time limits** — or the ability to extend them.

See `accessibility.skill.md` for comprehensive implementation guidance.

### Accessibility Testing Tools

| Tool | Cost | What It Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| axe DevTools (browser extension) | Free | Automated testing in Chrome/Edge/Firefox |
| WAVE (web tool) | Free | Visual accessibility report |
| Lighthouse (built into Chrome) | Free | Includes accessibility score |
| Pa11y (command line) | Free | Automated testing for CI/CD |
| Manual testing | Free | Keyboard navigation, screen reader testing |

Automated tools catch approximately 30–40% of accessibility issues. Manual
testing (especially with a screen reader) is essential for full coverage.

## Terms and Conditions / Terms of Service

If your website:

- Sells products or services.
- Allows user accounts or user-generated content.
- Provides a platform others interact with.

You should have Terms and Conditions covering:

- What the service is and who can use it.
- Payment terms (if applicable).
- Intellectual property (who owns what).
- Liability limitations.
- Account termination.
- Dispute resolution.
- Governing law (which country's law applies).

For simple brochure sites with a contact form only, Terms may not be necessary —
a Privacy Policy is usually sufficient.

## Data Collection Points to Audit

Every website should identify ALL points where personal data is collected:

| Collection Point | Data Collected | Consent Required? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contact form | Name, email, phone, message content | Privacy policy disclosure; GDPR may require consent |
| Newsletter signup | Email address | Explicit consent (all jurisdictions) |
| Analytics (cookie-based) | IP, device, pages visited, referrer | Consent in EU/UK; disclosure elsewhere |
| Analytics (cookie-free) | Aggregate data only (no personal data) | Usually no consent needed |
| eCommerce | Name, address, payment details, purchase history | Contract basis; privacy policy required |
| User accounts | Email, password, profile information | Consent or contract |
| Comments/reviews | Name/username, email, content | Privacy policy disclosure |
| Live chat | Name, email, conversation content | Privacy policy; possible consent |
| Social media embeds | IP, cookies, browsing data (sent to platform) | Consent in EU/UK |
| Google Maps embed | IP, location data | Consent in EU/UK |
| YouTube embed | IP, cookies, viewing data | Consent in EU/UK |

## Children's Data

If your website might be used by children (under 13 in the US, under 16 in
some EU countries):

| Law | Jurisdiction | Requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **COPPA** | USA | Verifiable parental consent before collecting data from children under 13 |
| **GDPR** | EU | Parental consent for children under 16 (member states can lower to 13) |
| **UK GDPR + Age Appropriate Design Code** | UK | Child-friendly design, minimal data collection, age verification |

If your site is not designed for children, state this explicitly in your privacy
policy. If children might use it anyway, additional safeguards are required.

## International Data Transfers

If you use services hosted outside your visitors' country (e.g. US-based hosting,
Google Analytics, Mailchimp), this constitutes an "international data transfer"
under GDPR and similar laws.

Requirements:

- Disclose the transfer in your privacy policy.
- Ensure adequate safeguards (Standard Contractual Clauses, adequacy decision,
  or equivalent).
- For EU→US transfers, the EU-US Data Privacy Framework currently provides a
  mechanism (verify current status as this changes).

## Compliance Checklist for Launch

Before going live:

- [ ] Identified which privacy laws apply based on visitor geography.
- [ ] Privacy policy written, published, and linked from every page.
- [ ] Cookie consent mechanism implemented (if required by applicable law).
- [ ] All data collection points identified and documented.
- [ ] Third-party services listed in privacy policy.
- [ ] Data processing agreements in place with third-party processors.
- [ ] Accessibility tested (automated + manual) against WCAG 2.1 AA.
- [ ] Cookie consent blocks non-essential cookies until explicit consent.
- [ ] "Do Not Sell" link added (if CPRA applies).
- [ ] Contact mechanism for data subject rights requests.
- [ ] Data retention periods defined.
- [ ] Someone is named as the compliance maintenance owner.
- [ ] Review date scheduled (at least annually).

## Ongoing Compliance Maintenance

| Frequency | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly | Check if new third-party scripts were added (each needs privacy policy mention) |
| Quarterly | Review cookie scan (new cookies from plugin updates?) |
| Biannually | Accessibility retest (especially after design changes) |
| Annually | Full compliance audit — have laws changed? New jurisdictions to consider? |
| On every change | If adding a new form, embed, analytics tool, or third-party service — update privacy policy |
| On legal changes | Monitor for law updates in applicable jurisdictions |

## When to Get Legal Advice

Consult a lawyer when:

- You process sensitive data (health, financial, children's, biometric).
- You operate in multiple jurisdictions with conflicting requirements.
- You receive a data subject access request and are unsure how to respond.
- You experience a data breach.
- Your business model involves selling or sharing personal data.
- You are unsure whether a specific practice is lawful.
- Penalties for non-compliance would materially harm your business.

---

## Critical Constraints

- Never assume privacy law doesn't apply because you're "too small" — many laws
  have no revenue threshold.
- Never use a privacy policy template without verifying it matches your actual
  data practices.
- Never load tracking cookies before consent in EU/UK jurisdictions.
- Never hide the "Reject" option in cookie banners.
- Never assume a country has no privacy law — check first.
- Never copy another site's privacy policy — yours must reflect YOUR practices.
- Never store more personal data than you need, or keep it longer than necessary.
- Always disclose ALL third-party services that receive visitor data.
- Always provide a working mechanism for data subject rights requests.
- Always test accessibility with keyboard navigation and a screen reader.
- Always review compliance annually — laws change frequently.
- Always document your compliance decisions for future reference.
