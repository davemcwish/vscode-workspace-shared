# Skill: Website Planning, Platform Choice, Social Presence, Launch, and Maintenance

## Purpose

Guide a beginner from a website idea to a live, maintainable website.

Assume the user may be minimally computer-literate and may have no prior
experience with website design, HTML, CSS, hosting, domains, CMS platforms,
accessibility, analytics, search engine optimization, social media, online
reviews, or digital marketing.

**Currentness warning:** Website platforms, hosting plans, domain rules, pricing,
social media features, analytics tools, privacy requirements, accessibility
expectations, payment options, advertising rules, search engine behaviour, and
launch best practices change over time. Before making final provider, legal,
privacy, compliance, or launch decisions, verify current details from official
sources and test the actual website.

---

## Core Principle

Do not start with code.

Do not start by choosing a platform either.

First understand:

- business purpose,
- target audience,
- country or region,
- legal business location,
- website type,
- primary conversion or success measure,
- social media and online presence,
- maintenance owner,
- content needs,
- privacy and accessibility needs,
- platform options,
- launch process.
- post-launch maintenance process,
- risk areas such as privacy, security, accessibility, claims, payments, and
  regulated content.

---

## Beginner Launch Workflow

Use this sequence for beginners:

1. **Clarify purpose** - what the website is for and what success looks like.
2. **Clarify audience** - who needs the site and what they are trying to do.
3. **Clarify geography** - country, region, service area, language, currency,
   tax, privacy, and support needs.
4. **Clarify ownership** - who owns the domain, platform, content, analytics,
   social profiles, and maintenance.
5. **Choose the simplest suitable platform** - do not over-engineer.
6. **Plan content** - pages, copy, images, calls to action, trust signals, and
   legal/policy content.
7. **Plan findability** - SEO basics, local SEO if relevant, social previews,
   business listings, and internal links.
8. **Plan safety and compliance** - privacy, cookies, accessibility, security,
   payments, claims, and regulated content.
9. **Build and test** - test on mobile, desktop, slow connections, and real user
   journeys.
10. **Launch carefully** - check domains, SSL, redirects, forms, analytics,
    search tools, and backups.
11. **Monitor after launch** - check errors, enquiries, analytics, search
    visibility, performance, and user feedback.
12. **Maintain** - assign owners and review content, security, privacy,
    performance, SEO, and social presence regularly.

## Ask for Missing Context First

If not provided, ask:

- What is the website for?
- What is the main goal: enquiries, bookings, sales, donations, information,
  recruitment, support, community, internal use, or something else?
- Who is the target audience?
- Which country, region, city, language, currency, and service area matter?
- Is the website public, internal, private, temporary, or permanent?
- Does the business already have a domain name?
- Does the business already have professional email?
- Who owns the domain, hosting, CMS, social profiles, analytics, and business
  listings?
- Who will update the website after launch?
- What pages are needed at launch?
- What content, photos, documents, policies, or legal text already exist?
- Does the site need forms, bookings, payments, downloads, accounts, maps,
  videos, chat, reviews, or integrations?
- Does the site need privacy, cookie, accessibility, security, payment, or
  regulated-content review?
- What is the budget and timeline?
- What technical skill level does the maintainer have?

---

## Important Distinction

There are two different kinds of HTML/CSS work in this project:

1. **Static reports** - generated files for local or internal viewing.
2. **Websites** - public or internal sites that may require platform choice,
   hosting, domains, accessibility, privacy, social presence, maintenance, and
   launch planning.

For static reports, prefer simple generated HTML/CSS.

For websites, do not start coding until strategy, audience, platform,
maintenance owner, geography, social media and online presence, UX, content, and
launch plan are clear.

---

## Minimum Viable Website

A beginner website does not need every possible feature at launch.

A simple launch version may only need:

- homepage,
- about page,
- service/product/offer page,
- contact page,
- privacy/cookie information where required,
- clear call to action,
- mobile-friendly layout,
- basic SEO titles and descriptions,
- analytics or measurement where appropriate,
- search engine submission where appropriate,
- maintenance owner.

Avoid adding complex features such as logins, payments, booking systems, live
chat, advertising pixels, embedded feeds, or automation unless there is a clear
business need and someone can maintain them.

## Country and Region-Specific Provider Consideration

Do not assume that US-based or globally popular platforms are automatically the
best choice.

When recommending a website platform, hosting provider, domain registrar,
payment provider, analytics tool, email marketing tool, or social media-related
integration, consider the user's country or region.

Ask:

- Which country or region is the website mainly for?
- Where is the business legally based?
- Where are most visitors expected to be?
- Does the user need local language support?
- Does the user need local currency support?
- Does the user need local tax, VAT, GST, or sales-tax support?
- Does the user need local payment provider support?
- Does the user need local customer support?
- Does the provider offer hosting or data storage in the required region?
- Are there privacy, cookie, accessibility, or legal-page expectations in that
  country or region?
- Is the provider commonly used and trusted in that market?

When local context matters, compare global platforms with relevant local or
regional alternatives instead of defaulting to Wix, Squarespace, WordPress, or
Shopify.

## Domain, Email, and Account Ownership

For beginners, ownership mistakes can create serious problems later.

Before launch, confirm:

- who owns the domain name,
- where the domain is registered,
- when the domain renews,
- who has access to DNS settings,
- whether professional email is needed,
- who owns the website platform account,
- who owns hosting or deployment access,
- who owns analytics and search console accounts,
- who owns social media and business listing accounts,
- whether recovery email addresses and phone numbers are current,
- whether more than one trusted person has recovery access where appropriate.

Beginner rule:

The business or organisation should own its domain, website, analytics, and key
profiles. Avoid leaving critical accounts under a freelancer's personal email
unless there is a clear written agreement and handover plan.

## Social Media and Online Presence Consideration

For many small businesses, the website is only one part of the customer journey.

The practical journey may be:

```text
Social media post
  -> Profile bio/link
  -> Website landing page
  -> Contact form / booking / purchase
  -> Follow-up by email, phone, CRM, or social message
```

Ask whether the business already uses or needs:

- Facebook
- Instagram
- TikTok
- LinkedIn
- Threads
- Bluesky
- X
- YouTube
- Pinterest
- WhatsApp Business
- Google Business Profile
- Apple Business Connect
- Yelp or other review platforms
- industry-specific directories or booking platforms

Do not assume every business needs every platform.

Do not recommend creating new social media accounts unless the business has the
time, content, audience need, and maintenance owner to keep them active.

Choose platforms based on:

- where the target audience already spends time,
- whether the business can maintain the platform consistently,
- whether the platform supports the business goal,
- whether the business has suitable content for that platform,
- whether the platform is common and trusted in the target country or region,
- whether privacy, moderation, safeguarding, or brand-safety concerns apply.

Ask or confirm:

- Does the business already have social media profiles?
- Which platforms are currently active?
- Which platforms actually bring enquiries, bookings, sales, or traffic?
- Who owns the login credentials?
- Who will post and respond to messages?
- How often can the business realistically post?
- Should the website link to social profiles?
- Should social profiles link back to the website?
- Is a "link in bio" page needed?
- Are social feeds needed on the website, or would simple profile links be safer?
- Are reviews, testimonials, or social proof important?
- Does the business need share images, Open Graph tags, or social preview text?
- Does the business need tracking parameters for social campaigns?
- Are there brand, privacy, safeguarding, moderation, or compliance concerns?

Prefer simple links to social profiles unless there is a clear reason to embed
live social feeds.

Embedded feeds can create:

- privacy issues,
- cookie issues,
- performance issues,
- accessibility issues,
- reliability issues,
- maintenance issues,
- distractions from the main call to action.

## Platform Recommendation Rules

Recommend the simplest sustainable platform that meets the real need. Do not
recommend a more complex platform just because it is more powerful.

The platform must fit:

- the user's objective,
- the target audience's needs,
- the maintainer's technical skill level,
- the country or region requirements,
- the content update frequency,
- the social media and online presence needs,
- the privacy and accessibility requirements,
- the budget and timeline,
- ownership and handover needs,
- backup/export options,
- support availability,
- security update responsibilities,
- integrations such as forms, bookings, payments, analytics, email marketing, or
  CRM where required.

Consider:

| Platform Type | Examples | Best For | Beginner Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No-code website builder | Wix, Squarespace, or trusted local equivalents | Simple brochure sites, portfolios, local businesses | Easiest for non-technical users |
| Content management system | WordPress or regionally popular CMS platforms | Content-heavy or growing sites | Flexible but needs maintenance |
| eCommerce platform | Shopify, WooCommerce, or local eCommerce platforms | Product catalogues, payments, tax, and shipping | Must support local payment, tax, and shipping needs |
| Local hosting provider | Country-specific hosting companies | Sites needing local support, local billing, or regional data hosting | Useful when local support and compliance matter |
| Static HTML/CSS | GitHub Pages, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, local hosting, or internal hosting | Simple fast developer-maintained sites | Low running cost but harder for non-technical updates |
| Custom web app | Cloud provider, local hosting provider, or managed application platform | Interactive workflows, dashboards, user accounts, integrations | Most flexible but highest cost and maintenance |
| Generated static report | Python-generated HTML/CSS report files | Read-only internal reporting or offline review | Not a full website and usually not intended for public launch |
| Local web app | Flask, FastAPI, or similar internal application | Internal workflows or tools that need interaction | Not ideal for a simple public marketing website |

---

## Platform Decision Output

When recommending a platform, explain:

- recommended platform type,
- why it fits the user's goal,
- why it fits the maintainer's skill level,
- country or region considerations,
- likely costs to verify,
- maintenance responsibilities,
- ownership and account setup needs,
- limitations or trade-offs,
- risks if the site grows,
- when to reconsider the decision.

Avoid saying "use WordPress", "use Wix", "use Shopify", or "build custom" without
explaining why that choice fits the specific situation.

## Pre-Launch Social Checks

Before launch, confirm:

- approved social media profiles are documented,
- website links to approved social media profiles,
- social media profiles link back to the website,
- business name, logo, contact details, and opening hours are consistent across
  website and social profiles,
- social sharing preview title, description, and image are checked,
- reviews, testimonials, or social proof are approved for use,
- embedded social feeds are avoided unless there is a clear reason to use them,
- any tracking pixels, social ads, or campaign links are approved and documented,
- someone owns social media updates and message responses after launch.

## Full Pre-Launch Website Checklist

Before launching a public website, confirm:

### Strategy and Ownership

- [ ] Website purpose is clear.
- [ ] Target audience is clear.
- [ ] Primary call to action is clear.
- [ ] Website owner is named.
- [ ] Maintenance owner is named.
- [ ] Domain owner is known.
- [ ] Platform, hosting, analytics, search, social, and listing account owners
      are known.

### Content

- [ ] Required launch pages are complete.
- [ ] Placeholder text has been removed.
- [ ] Contact details are correct.
- [ ] Prices, opening hours, services, locations, and policies are current.
- [ ] Claims, testimonials, reviews, photos, awards, and logos are approved for
      use.
- [ ] Regulated or high-risk claims have been reviewed where appropriate.

### Privacy, Legal, and Consent

- [ ] Privacy information is present where required.
- [ ] Cookie or tracking consent is handled where required.
- [ ] Forms explain what happens to submitted information.
- [ ] Email marketing consent is clear where used.
- [ ] Terms, returns, cancellation, refund, accessibility, or complaints
      information is present where relevant.

### Accessibility and Usability

- [ ] Site works on mobile and desktop.
- [ ] Navigation is clear.
- [ ] Text is readable.
- [ ] Links and buttons are descriptive.
- [ ] Images have appropriate alt text.
- [ ] Forms have labels and useful error messages.
- [ ] Important information is not only inside images, PDFs, videos, maps, or
      carousels.

### Performance

- [ ] Images are optimised.
- [ ] Fonts and scripts are limited.
- [ ] Third-party embeds are justified.
- [ ] Key pages load acceptably on mobile and slower networks.
- [ ] Performance baseline is recorded.

### Security

- [ ] HTTPS/SSL works.
- [ ] Admin accounts use strong passwords.
- [ ] Multi-factor authentication is enabled where available.
- [ ] Unused plugins, apps, themes, and accounts are removed.
- [ ] Backups or export options are understood.
- [ ] Forms, payments, and user data handling are checked.

### SEO and Findability

- [ ] Page titles and meta descriptions are reviewed.
- [ ] Headings are logical.
- [ ] Important pages are linked from navigation or content.
- [ ] Sitemap or platform equivalent is available where appropriate.
- [ ] Search engines are not accidentally blocked.
- [ ] Local SEO basics are checked where relevant.
- [ ] Social sharing previews are checked.

### Analytics and Measurement

- [ ] Analytics is configured where appropriate.
- [ ] Conversion actions are defined.
- [ ] Form submissions, bookings, purchases, calls, or key actions are tested.
- [ ] Google Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, or regional equivalents are
      considered for public search-driven sites.
- [ ] Internal traffic or test traffic is understood where relevant.

### Social and Local Presence

- [ ] Approved social profiles are linked.
- [ ] Business listings are accurate where relevant.
- [ ] Review platforms are monitored where relevant.
- [ ] Someone owns social messages and profile updates.

## Go-Live Process

Use a controlled launch process.

Recommended steps:

1. Record the current domain, DNS, hosting, and website settings.
2. Confirm who can make DNS or platform changes.
3. Take a backup or export where the platform supports it.
4. Publish during a low-risk time if the website is important to operations.
5. Enable HTTPS/SSL.
6. Connect the domain.
7. Check the homepage and key pages.
8. Test forms, bookings, payments, downloads, maps, and contact links.
9. Test on mobile and desktop.
10. Check that analytics and conversion tracking work where used.
11. Check that search engines are not blocked.
12. Submit or verify sitemap/search tools where appropriate.
13. Check social sharing previews.
14. Check business listings and social profile links.
15. Record the launch date, platform, owners, and baseline results.

For important sites, have a rollback plan:

- previous site backup,
- DNS settings before launch,
- platform support contact,
- developer or maintainer contact,
- list of critical pages and forms to test,
- decision point for reverting if something major fails.

## Post-Launch Monitoring

A website launch is not finished when the site appears online.

During the first 24 - 48 hours, check:

- website loads correctly,
- HTTPS works,
- forms arrive in the right inbox,
- booking or payment flows work,
- contact links work,
- analytics records visits where used,
- search engines are not blocked,
- key pages work on mobile,
- social profile links work,
- business listings point to the correct website,
- no obvious broken links or missing images exist.

During the first month, check:

- real enquiries, bookings, sales, or other goals,
- search visibility,
- analytics trends,
- common user questions,
- form spam or suspicious activity,
- page speed,
- accessibility or usability feedback,
- social and local listing messages,
- reviews and reputation,
- content corrections or missing information.

Schedule the first post-launch review before launch day.

---

## Ongoing Website Maintenance

Assign maintenance ownership before launch.

| Frequency | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Weekly during first month | Check forms, enquiries, analytics, search visibility, broken links, and obvious errors |
| Monthly | Review content accuracy, contact details, opening hours, offers, prices, and service information |
| Monthly | Check analytics, conversions, and search visibility where configured |
| Monthly | Review form spam, suspicious activity, failed messages, or unusual traffic |
| Quarterly | Review platform, theme, plugin, app, integration, and account access risks |
| Quarterly | Review accessibility, performance, SEO, privacy, security, and content quality |
| Quarterly | Check social profiles, business listings, reviews, and local information |
| Annually | Review platform fit, costs, ownership, domain renewal, legal pages, privacy approach, and maintenance process |
| On every business change | Update affected pages, listings, social profiles, forms, policies, and tracking |
| On every platform or provider change | Re-test key pages, forms, analytics, search visibility, performance, and security |

A maintainable website is better than an impressive website that nobody can
update safely.

## Current Information and Verification Rules

If recommending specific hosting providers, website builders, CMS platforms,
domain registrars, payment providers, analytics tools, email marketing tools,
booking tools, advertising platforms, social media platforms, review platforms,
business listing platforms, privacy tools, accessibility tools, pricing,
availability, data-hosting regions, API access, verification rules, or compliance
features, verify the latest information from official provider sources before
making a final recommendation.

Do not present provider pricing, platform features, legal requirements,
advertising rules, social platform behaviour, search engine behaviour, or
compliance features as permanent facts.

Where current details matter, say what must be verified and where the user should
verify it.

---

## Critical Constraints

- Never start with code before understanding purpose, audience, platform,
  ownership, content, geography, launch process, and maintenance.
- Never recommend a platform without considering the maintainer's skill level.
- Never recommend a platform without considering country, region, payment,
  privacy, accessibility, support, and legal expectations where relevant.
- Never assume every business needs social media, eCommerce, analytics, ads,
  live chat, embedded feeds, or automation.
- Never launch with placeholder content.
- Never launch without testing forms, contact links, bookings, payments, and
  other critical user journeys.
- Never launch a public site without checking HTTPS.
- Never ignore privacy, accessibility, security, performance, SEO, content
  accuracy, and maintenance ownership.
- Never leave domain, hosting, analytics, social, or listing ownership unclear.
- Never recommend fake reviews, fake locations, misleading claims, or fake
  urgency.
- Always choose the simplest sustainable option that meets the real need.
- Always name a maintenance owner before launch.
- Always schedule a post-launch review.
