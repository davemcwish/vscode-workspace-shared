# Workflow: Website Idea to Live Launch

## Purpose

Use this workflow when a user wants to create a public or internal website and
needs beginner-friendly guidance from initial idea to live launch.

## Target User

Assume the user:

- may not know HTML or CSS,
- may not understand domains or hosting,
- may not know what a CMS is,
- may not understand social media's role in a website project,
- may need help choosing between no-code tools, WordPress, Shopify, local
  providers, static HTML/CSS, or a custom web app.

## Step 1: Strategy

Clarify:

- objective,
- target audience,
- primary call to action,
- success measures,
- maintenance owner.

## Step 2: Geographic Scope

Clarify whether the site is:

- global,
- regional,
- country-specific,
- state/province/county-specific,
- local.

Record any implications for:

- language,
- currency,
- privacy,
- cookies,
- accessibility,
- legal pages,
- payment methods,
- tax, VAT, GST, or sales tax,
- hosting,
- data storage,
- local customer support,
- local trust signals.

## Step 3: Social Media and Online Presence

Clarify whether the business already uses or needs:

- Facebook,
- Instagram,
- TikTok,
- LinkedIn,
- Threads,
- Bluesky,
- X,
- YouTube,
- Pinterest,
- WhatsApp Business,
- Google Business Profile,
- Apple Business Connect,
- review platforms,
- industry-specific directories,
- booking platforms.

Ask:

- Which profiles already exist?
- Which profiles are active?
- Which profiles bring enquiries, bookings, sales, traffic, or trust?
- Who owns the login credentials?
- Who will post updates?
- Who will respond to messages?
- Should the website link to social profiles?
- Should social profiles link back to the website?
- Are reviews, testimonials, or social proof needed?
- Is a "link in bio" page needed?
- Are social sharing previews needed?
- Are social ads, campaign links, or tracking pixels planned?
- Are the social profiles accurate and up to date?
- Are business name, logo, opening hours, address, phone number, and email
  consistent across social profiles and the website?


Prefer simple links to social profiles unless embedded feeds have a clear
business reason.

Record any privacy, cookie, accessibility, performance, moderation, safeguarding,
or brand-safety risks.

## Step 4: Platform Decision

Compare:

| Platform | Best Fit | Main Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No-code website builder such as Wix, Squarespace, or trusted local equivalents | Fast beginner-managed site | Limited advanced customization |
| WordPress or another CMS | Flexible content-heavy site | Requires updates, backups, plugin management, and security maintenance |
| Shopify, WooCommerce, or local eCommerce platform | Online stores, payments, tax, and shipping | Less ideal for non-store sites or unsupported local payment/tax needs |
| Local or regional hosting provider | Local support, billing, data hosting, or country-specific trust | Quality varies; support, security, backups, and SSL must be checked |
| Static HTML/CSS | Simple fast developer-managed site | Harder for non-technical updates |
| Generated static report | Read-only internal reports | Not a full public website |
| Local web app | Internal interactive workflow | Not ideal for a simple public marketing site |
| Custom app | Complex workflows, accounts, dashboards, or integrations | Highest cost and support burden |

Choose the simplest sustainable platform.

The platform decision must consider:

- user skill level,
- maintenance owner,
- country or region,
- local provider options,
- social media and online presence needs,
- local payment and tax needs,
- privacy and cookie expectations,
- accessibility expectations,
- budget,
- timeline.

## Step 5: UX/UI Design

Define:

- visual hierarchy,
- navigation,
- page layout,
- call to action,
- mobile-first behavior,
- desktop/tablet/phone support,
- design system,
- accessibility,
- performance target,
- trust signals,
- reviews and testimonials,
- social profile links,
- social sharing previews.

## Step 6: Content Plan

Create:

- site map,
- page list,
- text draft,
- image list,
- contact details,
- legal/privacy requirements,
- frequently asked questions,
- approved social profile links,
- reviews or testimonials,
- social proof,
- share-preview title,
- share-preview description,
- share-preview image.

## Step 7: Build

Follow platform-specific guidance.

For HTML/CSS:

- create semantic HTML,
- create responsive CSS,
- avoid unnecessary JavaScript,
- add approved social profile links,
- add share-preview metadata where appropriate,
- test in multiple browsers and devices,
- validate accessibility basics.

For CMS, no-code, or hosted platforms:

- configure pages,
- configure navigation,
- add content,
- configure social profile links,
- configure share-preview settings,
- configure forms,
- configure analytics if approved,
- configure cookie or privacy settings if needed.

## Step 8: Pre-Launch Review

Check:

- links,
- forms,
- spelling,
- mobile layout,
- browser compatibility,
- accessibility,
- privacy/cookie content,
- analytics,
- page titles,
- metadata,
- social sharing previews,
- social profile links,
- business information consistency,
- SSL,
- domain setup,
- link previews have been tested on major relevant social platforms,
- approved social profile URLs are correct,
- old or unused social profiles are not linked accidentally.

Also confirm:

- approved social profiles are documented,
- website links to approved social profiles,
- social profiles link back to the website,
- business name, logo, contact details, and opening hours are consistent,
- embedded social feeds are avoided unless clearly justified,
- tracking pixels or social ad integrations are approved and documented,
- someone owns social media updates and message responses after launch.

## Step 9: Launch

Publish the site, connect the domain, verify SSL, test live pages, and confirm
analytics, forms, social links, and social sharing previews work.

## Step 10: Handover

Document:

- how to update content,
- who owns the site,
- who owns each social media profile,
- how to monitor form submissions,
- how to monitor messages or orders,
- how to update social profile links,
- how to check reviews or testimonials,
- how backups work,
- when to review content,
- when to review social media links,
- who to contact for technical issues.

## Done Checklist

- [ ] Website objective is clear.
- [ ] Target audience is clear.
- [ ] Country and region needs are clear.
- [ ] Social media and online presence have been reviewed.
- [ ] Platform choice is justified.
- [ ] Site map is approved.
- [ ] Content plan is approved.
- [ ] UX/UI needs are documented.
- [ ] Accessibility basics are covered.
- [ ] Social profile links are approved.
- [ ] Social sharing previews are checked.
- [ ] Privacy and cookie needs are checked.
- [ ] Domain and SSL are working.
- [ ] Forms are tested.
- [ ] Analytics are tested if used.
- [ ] Handover owner is identified.
- [ ] Social media owner is identified.
- [ ] Business details are consistent across website and approved social profiles.
- [ ] Website-to-social and social-to-website links are tested.
- [ ] Embedded social feeds are avoided unless justified.
- [ ] Tracking pixels or social ad integrations are approved if used.
